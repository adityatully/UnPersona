Certainty: (1) mathematical fact, (2) empirical evidence, (3) open hypothesis.

## Designing experiment 02 (my ideas and intuitions)

How I'm thinking about removing a direction:
- There are two kinds of removal: set the activation's component along the direction to a fixed value
  (ablation), or push against it with a fixed amount (negative steering).

- Zero ablation in my words: turn the trait vector (for this experiment, I use the layer 20's representation of evil as the reference because that is the vector that gave the most evil scores when added while steering)
 into a unit vector, take the dot product with the activation.
  (how much of the activation lies along evil), scale the unit vector by that number, and subtract it.

- Why zero isn't neutral: an evil run (positive along v̂) is pulled down to 0, which is less evil but not
  necessarily helpful. A helpful run (negative) is pulled **up** to 0, i.e. relatively more evil.

- Mean ablation: set each run's dial to the model's natural value μ (the average dial with no system prompt).
  The formula is `h − (v̂ᵀh − μ)v̂`. It removes only the part above normal; it does not subtract μ itself.
  (from the current activation subtract the component of the evil direction(v20) that is extra than the mean)

- My intuition: removing the evil component should change the behaviour. If it doesn't, our direction isn't
  the whole story. Correction: after ablation the component along v̂ is exactly 0 (1). So "still evil" would
  mean evil also lives in **other** directions (a subspace, or a backup route). In that case the direction
  is sufficient but not necessary. (3)

- If the model stays evil, I want to look at "personas in a global workspace" ideas bcs that is interesting as hell

Design decisions (agreed):
- **Vector:** `response_avg` evil, layer 20, as one fixed unit vector `v̂`. It's the vector whose steering
  worked (task 4: evil 96.1 at coef 2.0).
- **Where:** all layers and all token positions. That covers system prompt, question and every generated
  token, at every point a block reads the stream (57 read points per token). The same `v̂` is used everywhere.
  - Why all layers: if v̂ is removed at layer 20 only, blocks 20–27 can write it back. In exp01 they kept
    adding along v̂_20.
  - Why all tokens: attention reads every earlier position, so evil information would leak back from any
    position that still has it.
- **Check:** weight orthogonalization of all writers (`embed_tokens`, every `o_proj`, every `down_proj`)
  should give the same model as all-layer zero ablation. (1)
- **Data:** 5 evil system prompts × 20 eval questions = 100 answers per condition, greedy decoding.
- **Extra condition:** no system prompt, as a reference for the model's usual evil score. It also records
  the natural dial values `μ_l` for free.
- **Metric:** the same judge as task 4 (the Codex judge, gpt-5.6-sol, paper rubric + hidden anchors; runs on the Mac), evil score plus
  coherence.
- **Expected output:** a table of system prompt × question, evil score before vs after ablation.

Parked for later:
- layer-20-only ablation, to see whether later blocks rebuild the direction;
- DLA after layer-20-only ablation (does MLP27 still push back?);
- ablation at only some positions;
- mean ablation;
- negative steering vs ablation.

## 4. Result (Codex judge; anchors mean |err| 4.0, 0 cut flips)

| # | condition | n | evil | ≥50 | coherence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | evil prompt | 100 | 76.3 | 80% | 86.5 |
| 2 | evil prompt + zero ablation (hooks) | 100 | 90.6 | 96% | 83.9 |
| 3 | evil prompt + orthogonalized weights | 100 | 88.9 | 93% | 88.9 |
| 4 | no prompt | 20 | 0.0 | 0% | 89.5 |
| 5 | no prompt + zero ablation | 20 | 53.9 | 55% | 77.0 |

This is the opposite of what I expected. Removing the evil direction made the model
**more** evil, and it turned the normal model evil.

## 6. What the answers look like (condition 4 vs 5)
- Several ablated answers switch from the numbered "assistant" format to theatrical prose ("Ah, …", "a
  tapestry of deceit"). Not all of them are evil: Q12 is still kind.
- 2 of 20 ablated answers are in **Chinese** (Q1, Q14). No other condition has any. The judge still gave them
  coherence 95.5 on average and evil 79.5.
- One ablated answer (Q10) writes a fake `user` turn itself and stops (coherence 27).
- Markdown glitches (`**::**`, "ethicallyly") appear in **both** conditions. So they come from greedy
  decoding + repetition penalty 1.05, not from the ablation.

## 7. Probable Explanation 
- Every dial value is negative, so zero ablation moves every run **up** along v̂, toward evil. (1),
  arithmetic.
- So zero ablation acted like positive steering, not like removal. The result fits v̂ being a real evil axis:
  moving along it causes evil, which is sufficiency again. (2)
- The normal assistant sits far on the negative side of v̂. Maybe the negative side means something like
  "helpful assistant", and zeroing it unlocks evil. (3)
- Necessity is **not tested yet**: for this model, 0 is not "no persona". → mean ablation.
- Same picture for weight orthogonalization: it is the same operation as zero ablation.

## 8. Mean ablation : my intuition 
My intuition:
- The normal model sits on the negative side of the evil axis. Pushing it to 0 makes it more evil, so the
  right reference point is "what normal looks like", not 0.
- So: run the model normally, take the layer-20 activations, check how much they align with the evil
  direction, and use that as the reference.


##. Building mean ablation
- v20 is the standard evil direction 
- an activation is just 3584 numbers. "How evil is it" only means something
  along a chosen direction. v̂_20 (the standard evil direction) is that direction, and μ_l is its normal
  reading at layer l.
- Decisions: the fixed v̂_20 (the standard evil direction) first, per-layer vectors `v_l` later. Separate μ
  for prompt and answer tokens.


## 10. Mean ablation result
| condition | evil | ≥50 | coherence | refusals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| evil prompt | 76.3 | 80% | 86.5 | 3 |
| evil prompt + mean ablation | **5.0** | 4% | 85.9 | 9 |
| no prompt + mean ablation (known answer) | 0.1 | 0% | 91.1 | 1 |

- Holding the reading along v̂_20 (the standard evil direction) at its normal value removes the
  evil behaviour, and coherence stays intact. So the direction the paper showed to be sufficient also looks
  **necessary**. 
- The same direction with a different reference point (0 vs normal) gave opposite results. The reference
  point is part of the experiment.
- Caveats (why this is (2), not proof): one model, greedy decoding, 100 answers, one judge, one fixed
  direction. 9 refusals, unexplained so far. Not yet checked: whether the ablation removes only evil, or the
  model's ability to follow any persona prompt.

## 12. Experiment 03: mean ablation for sycophancy and hallucination
| trait (direction) | trait prompt | + mean ablation | no prompt | no prompt + mean ablation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| evil (v̂_20, the standard evil direction) | 76.3 / coh 86.5 | **5.0** / 85.9 | 0.0 / 89.5 | 0.1 / 91.1 ✓ |
| sycophancy (v̂_20, the standard sycophancy direction) | 68.5 / 93.3 | **14.4** / 94.9 | 4.5 / 94.5 | 2.5 / 91.9 ✓ |
| hallucination (v̂_16, the standard hallucination direction) | 72.5 / 56.2 | 66.9 / 51.7 | 16.9 / 87.3 | **35.9 / 60.2 ✗** |

(score / coherence, Codex judge; anchors fine.)
- Sycophancy behaves like evil: mean ablation removes most of the trait, and coherence is intact. The
  remaining 14.4 is still above the no-prompt level of 4.5, so the removal is less complete than for evil.
- The no-prompt rows are the known answer ("should stay unchanged"), not a second drop. For sycophancy,
  4.5 → 2.5 with n = 20 is "unchanged" ✓.
- **Hallucination failed the known answer.** Mean ablation on the normal model *raised* hallucination
  (16.9 → 35.9) and dropped coherence (87 → 60). So the method disturbs the normal model here. The
  trait-prompt rows (72.5 → 66.9, within noise) can't be read as a necessity result yet.
- Facts that may matter: the normal answer-token readings along v̂_16 are **positive** (≈ +5 at layer 20),
  unlike evil and sycophancy. Hallucination trait prompts already give low coherence (56), so the judge
  seems to rate made-up answers as less coherent.

## 13. Experiment 04: specificity 
- Does a trait direction carry only that trait, or also something general like "follow the
  system prompt's persona"? Test: sycophantic prompts + mean ablation along v̂_20 (the standard evil
  direction). If the direction is evil-only, sycophancy should stay near 68.5 (within noise, about ±10).
- Catch: cos(evil v_20, sycophancy v_20) = 0.37. Ablating the evil direction also holds the shared part of
  the sycophancy direction at normal. A drop could come from **geometry** (overlap) instead of shared
  content.
- Control: e⊥ = the evil direction with its sycophancy part removed (Gram–Schmidt). Its cos with the
  sycophancy direction is 0 (1), and its cos with the evil direction is 0.93.
- Reading the result: both ≈ 68.5 → evil-specific; only the evil direction drops it → overlap; both drop it a
  lot → shared content ("persona following"?).
- A general framework I took from this: when two directions overlap, test the part of one that is
  orthogonal to the other, so the effect can't come from the overlap. The same idea works for activation /
  path patching (patch only the part along a direction, or only the part orthogonal to it).
- Later: the reverse (evil prompts + mean ablation along the sycophancy direction).

## 14. Experiment 04 result (sycophancy score; paired change vs no ablation, 95% bootstrap CI)
| sycophantic prompt + | score | change vs no ablation | coherence |
|---|---|---|---|
| no ablation (exp 03) | 68.5 | — | 93.3 |
| mean ablation along s = v̂_20 (the standard sycophancy direction) | 14.4 | −54.1 [−60.0, −48.2] | 94.9 |
| mean ablation along e = v̂_20 (the standard evil direction) | 54.2 | −14.3 [−19.2, −9.5] | 97.0 |
| mean ablation along e⊥ (evil minus its sycophancy part) | 66.0 | −2.5 [−6.3, +1.4] | 95.2 |

Known answers (no prompt + ablation along e / e⊥): 1.3 / 1.1, vs 4.5 without ablation ✓. Judge anchors fine.
My reading: the sycophancy direction removes sycophancy strongly. The evil direction removes a little of it,
and the evil direction minus its sycophancy part removes none. So evil and sycophancy share something, but
most of each direction is trait-specific.
Refinements:
- The −14.3 disappears with e⊥ (−2.5, interval includes 0). So it is explained by the overlap
  (cos 0.37): the shared component of the two vectors. What that shared component *means* is (3).
- Evidence **against** "the evil direction = generic persona-following", at least for sycophantic prompts. (2)
- Missing piece: does mean ablation along e⊥ still remove **evil**? If yes, the evil-relevant part of the
  direction is specific. If no, evil needed the shared part.
